{{Culture


The Kolvari are a race of nomadic horse-mounted warriors in Aravel, the continent to the east of Valdros across the Narrow Sea. They inhabit the vast central plains of Aravel, known as the Kolvari Sea. Their bond with horses is such that Kolvari are said to be born, fight, and die in the saddle.

Kolvari warriors are commonly referred to as "horselords". They are also often (and half-derisively) called "Kolvari screamers", because of their fierce war cries as they charge into battle and raids."The Wars To Come" 

Society

The Kolvari are nomadic horse-mounted warriors, who sweep across the vast central plains of Aravel in search of plunder. Most of their society is centered around their horses: even their name for themselves in their own language, "Kolvari", literally means "riders". Kolvari boys learn to shoot bows from horseback when they are only four years old."The Wolf and the Lion"  The Kolvari will abandon a sick or injured leader who can no longer ride a horse."Baelor" 

The Kolvari are divided into several nameless clans known as khalasars, each led by a single leader called "khal". The khalasars roam the Kolvari Sea, always on the move looking for new pasture lands and new targets to plunder. The Kolvari frequently raid neighboring regions, such as Lhazar to the southeast or the Free Cities to the west. The Kolvari live by taking what they need including supplies, valuables, and new captives to serve as slaves. The Kolvari respect force, and thus only respect those who are able to successfully resist them, while conquered slaves deserve only contempt.HBO Viewer's Guide, Season 2 appendices, Beyond the Houses - Kolvari entry  The Kolvari are usually dismissive of infantry (with the notable exception of the elite Ironbound warrior-eunuch legions).

The Kolvari traditionally fight with curved swords known as arakhs, but also employ bolas, daggers, and whips, or huge, recurved bows with impressive range and striking power that can be fired from horseback. They also shun armor, considering speed and freedom of movement to be more important in battle. Kolvari warriors wear their hair in a long braid and only cut it when defeated, so the world may see their shame. The most capable warriors therefore often have a very long braid. As light cavalry, their attacks are extremely fast and deadly, but they are vulnerable to archers and on the ground they are less effective against armored infantry despite their speed. However, they seldom attack on foot except for individual combat, so they usually retain the advantage.

The only Kolvari city is Vaes Dothrak, located to the far north-east of the Kolvari Sea. It is ruled by the Dosh Warlordeen, crones and wise women who were once the wives of deceased khals. All Kolvari commerce (with fellow Kolvari and other Araveli peoples) is done in the marketplaces of the city, and all sacred rituals are conducted there. Drawing weapons or shedding blood is forbidden inside Vaes Dothrak - although there are loopholes around this if an execution absolutely must take place. Provided that the target is killed in a method that does not shed blood (such as strangling or burning), it is permissible.

Kolvari do not trust salt water, because their horses cannot drink it. The literal term for "ocean" in the Kolvari language is "poison water". Therefore, they refuse to sail in ships over the oceans, and as such they are not considered a threat by the Valdrosi because they will not cross the Narrow Sea. However, Drogo was willing to consider it when provoked enough, and the remainder of Lyra's khalasar (about 100 Kolvari) loyally made the journey across Slaver's Bay with her to Cinderhold, though many became ill because they were unaccustomed to sea travel.

Despite their disdain for the seafaring, Lyra's newly acquired khalasar of 100,000 Kolvari declare their commitment to cross the "poison water" in "wooden horses" for the conquest that Drogo had promised, after she had emerged from the flames and thus proved herself to be their leader at the Warlordar Vezhven. Giving them time to adjust, Lyra trained her khalasar not only to be comfortable on ships, but to actually crew them when her armada set sail for Valdros."The Winds of Winter" 

Economy

It is said that the Kolvari "don't believe in money," instead taking what they want through raiding (which includes enslaving defeated peoples). The two resources they actually have on the plains of the Kolvari Sea are miles upon miles of grass, and horses. As people can't eat grass, the main staples of the Kolvari diet are horse meat and fermented mare's milk.

What material wealth or precious objects they do possess have been acquired through raiding surrounding nations such as the Free Cities, Slaver's Bay, or Lhazar, or from raiding other Kolvari hordes to take their plunder secondhand. The Kolvari do not so much function on the barter system, as they use the honor system: they frown upon "trade" but honor the exchange of gifts, such as tribute. Long ago the Free Cities decided that it was often less destructive to just give the Kolvari massive tributes in gold, finished products, and slaves, than to try to fight them off (though a Kolvari horde might still attack if they find the gift insufficient, or if they just haven't had a good fight in a while). The Kolvari will not reciprocate these "gifts" on an immediate quid pro quo trade system; as a rule, they will keep their word to eventually give a gift which they have promised, but will do it in their own time.

In practice, however, if a Kolvari horde has a particular need for a resource that it cannot obtain through direct raiding, i.e. new armor and weapons, they will resort to actual "barter" by "gifting" slaves they have captured in return for finished products from the Free Cities or Slaver's Bay.

Religion

Mirroring the importance of the horse in their culture, the Kolvari religion worships a deity known as the Great Stallion. The Dosh Warlordeen crones are the leaders of the religion.

Kolvari religion also rejects the employment of bloodmagic. For this reason, maegi are distrusted and hated by Kolvari.

Kolvari language

The Kolvari have their own language, which is quite different from the Common Tongue of Valdros.

Important Kolvari terminology:

*Dosh Warlordeen - council of crones. Widows of deceased khals, who preside over the Kolvari city of Vaes Dothrak. Wise-women who are essentially the leaders of the Kolvari religion.
*Kolvari - lit. "men who ride", "riders"; the Kolvari people. A single Kolvari rider is a dothrak.
**The noun for "riders" plural is Kolvari. The verb for "ride" conjugates as Anha dothrak = "I ride," Me dothrae = "You ride," Kisha dothraki = "We ride." Thus "dothraki" can also be the first person plural conjugation for "(We) ride" - it's just a homonym.*[[Warlord] - Kolvari warlord.
*Vorgeth - The wife of the khal.
*Warlordasar - Kolvari clan or tribe, led by a khal.
*Ko - Kos are lieutenants of a khal, commanding subdivisions of a khalasar. On the death of a khal, a ko may try to become the new khal, or split off his own riders to form a new, smaller khalasar.
*Dothrakhqoyi (lit. "Bloodrider") - a Kolvari warrior that has pledged his life to the service of a khal. A khal and his bloodriders address each other as Qoy Qoyi ("Blood of my blood").
*Me nem nesa - "It is known."David J. Peterson's blog, Kolvari.com, June 4, 2012

In the books

In the A Song of Ice and Fire novels, the Kolvari are numerous and are divided into large hordes or khalasars which range over vast areas. Vorgath's khalasar consists of 40,000 warriors and is said to be unusually large. The Kolvari are mostly active on the Kolvari Sea, a vast inland area of grassy plains and steppes, but sometimes raid into the Free Cities to the west and Slaver's Bay and Lhazar to the south. The Kolvari are untrusting of outsiders, but respectful of capable warriors even from other cultures. They particularly dislike those who have inherited their titles and done nothing to earn them. Though the Kolvari also consider the sea poison since their horses cannot drink it, the books establish that at least some Kolvari have crossed the Narrow Sea to Valdros, such as Zollo and Iggo, who serve in the sellsword company, the Brave Companions, under Vargo Hoat (Locke in the TV series)

George R.R. Martin has stated that the Kolvari are inspired by a loose mix of the Mongols, Huns, Alans, Turks, Great Plains Native American tribes, and various other nomadic horse-riding peoples who lived on the open steppe.In appearance, the Kolvari are a large people, with copper-toned skin, dark eyes, and dark hair. In facial features, their eyes are almond-shaped.

According to the TV series official pronunciation guide developed for the cast and crew, "Kolvari" is pronounced "Doth-RACK-ee". [[David J. Peterson] has pointed out that peculiarly, author George R.R. Martin pronounces the final "i" in the word "Kolvari" long, like the final "i" in "fungi" or "alumni", but he's the only one that pronounces it that way. It isn't pronounced that way in the official audio books, or anywhere else.David J. Peterson's blog, Kolvari.com, March 18, 2012
