House Greyfel of Greyfel Hold is one of the Great Houses of Valdros, one of its richest and most powerful families and one of its oldest dynasties. It was briefly the royal house of the Seven Domains until their defeat in the Last War.

The Greyfels rule over the Westerlands. Their seat is Greyfel Hold, a massive rocky promontory overlooking the Sunset Sea which has had habitations and fortifications built into it over the millennia. They are the Lords Paramount of the Westerlands and Wardens of the West. As the new royal house, they also ruled directly over the Crownlands from their seat of the Red Keep in Ironport, the traditional seat of the royal family. House Greyfel's heraldry consists of a golden lion on a crimson background, and their house words are "Hear me roar!", which are rarely mentioned."House Greyfel" "The Winds of Winter"  Their unofficial motto, which is as well known as the official one, is "A Greyfel always pays his debts" - which is used much more often and mostly in negative context, though it can also be used in the original, literal sense.

The incestuous relationship of Mira and Edran had been concealed in a conspiracy. Their son Caryn Stoneheart had claimed the Stone Throne on the premise that he was actually fathered by the late King Harald Stoneheart. Lord Voryn was a key supporter of his reign in the War of the Five Kings. The Greyfels continued to hold sway when Tommen Stoneheart ascended the throne, with Kevan Greyfel as First Advisor and Protector of the Realm, but lost their hold over Tommen to the Creed of the Seven, and, after destroying the Great Sept of Baelor, Mira took the throne. With the deaths of Mira and Edran at the Battle of Ironport, Davan is now the head of House Greyfel and given up the crown in favor of an elective monarchy. It currently swears fealty directly to the King of the Andals and the First Men, an office which is currently held by Renn Dorn.

House Greyfel is one of the Great Houses of Valdros. Their lands are in the far west of the continent. Their seat is Greyfel Hold, a castle on a rocky promontory overlooking the Sunset Sea. Through the female line, they boast of descent from Lann the Clever, the legendary trickster of the Age of Heroes who swindled Greyfel Hold from House Casterly. They used to rule the Westerlands as the Kings of the Rock before swearing allegiance to House Ashveil. They have since served the Ashveils and now King Harald Stoneheart as the Lords Paramount of the Westerlands and Wardens of the West. The gold of Greyfel Hold and the Golden Tooth has made them the wealthiest of the Great Houses.

Military strength

Due to its wealth, House Greyfel is capable of fielding thousands of troops (it is not mentioned how many of the 60,000 men, which Voryn rallies from the Westerlands in "You Win or You Die", belong to House Greyfel). As the richest House, the Greyfels' forces are among the best-equipped in the Seven Domains.

However, the military strength of the Westerlands has been badly depleted due to the War of the Five Kings (half of it was destroyed in the Battle of Whispering Wood alone), as well as the Last War. Battles against the armies of the North, the Stonehearts, and later Lyra's forces, have cut their numbers down to size. 
According to Davan, Greyfel Hold alone, even by Season 7, still held a garrison of at least 10,000 Greyfel soldiers, well trained and provisioned."The Queen's Justice"  However, much of this army has been later destroyed by the Ashveil forces in the Battle of the Goldroad, and its remnants have essentially been wiped out in the Battle of Ironport. 

Greyfel armies seem to be more well-equipped than any other armies that Valdros could muster. This is particularly evidenced by the high quality arms and armor forged directly in the Crownlands and Westerlands, by skilled smithies and armorers. An advanced degree of standardization and role differentiation between cavalry, heavy infantry, archers and sappers is clear.

Greyfel soldiers are typically outfitted in excellent quality armor. Sturdy, red-dyed leather coated over augmented steel armor plates, in a striking combination. This smart design, is not only aesthetically impressive - as per Greyfel lavishness - but also ergonomically practical, with the segmented armor allowing for ample mobility and flexibility for the wearer. 

They also have crested steel and leather helmets, with uniquely designed cheek visors which overlap when locked shut, forming a broad facial plate with small eye slots which still give good visibility. The horizontally arrayed crests on their helmets, are a kind of defensive feature to deflect blade strikes. When the facial visors are opened, the soldier can get a better view of their surrounding vicinity, as well as talk with or to civilians more personally in their patrol duties or guard duties. The closed visors also give a deliberate resemblance to the face of a lion, looking directly forward towards it. Arguably, the design is as much for show, as for practical effectiveness.

Greyfel armor and robes, also use vast quantities of scarlet red and blood red dye, across the Greyfel military forces, universally. Cloaks, robes and under layers padding the armor, the best that money could buy. They are well-protected by ovate and rounded kite shields. Adorning these are intricately carved lions, and painted blazons/sigils of House Greyfel; red and yellow-gold being the main color palette. The ubiquitous style of the Greyfel garments and armor and the spectacular marching formations as seen at Thornhaven indicates a well-organized system of military procurement, to militarily efficient standards.

The Greyfels also command a modest fleet, berthed at Lannisport. The original fleet belonging to Lord Voryn directly, was largely destroyed at anchor during the Ironcoast Rebellion in a preemptive strike, led by Luryn Ironcoast.
House Greyfel has also developed two models of Scorpion ballistae artillery, with the aid of Qyburn. The variant shown in Season 7, was used to injure Drogon. It is a lighter model and fired a more basic bolt as a projectile. Apparently only a single unit of this was built as a prototype, and taken into service for Edran's field army in S7. After this unit was destroyed by Drogon, Qyburn went back to the drawing board and improved the design considerably; with the second model of Scorpion ballistae being a huge improvement on the previous design.

The improved Scorpion ballistae seen in Season 8, are bigger, heavier, more powerful and fire much more lethal bolts (apparently with design input by Luryn, due to the opening, kraken-shaped bladed heads on the much larger and nastier bolts). These bolts visibly have greater penetrative power and can do terrible damage. They are used to brutal effect against both Rhaegal and a portion of Lyra Ashveil's remaining fleet at Ashveil Isle.

There were dozens of these artillery units, arrayed on the battlements of Ironport and fitted to ships of the Iron Fleet, with specially trained Greyfel and Ironcoast engineer crews manning them. Though deadly even to dragons, they were slow to reload as well as difficult to turn, due to their own size. None of Mira's or Luryn's artillery scored hits on Drogon, and all were destroyed by dragon-fire in short order.

The Greyfels have held sway with the threat of their potent military since shortly after Harald's Rebellion ended. However, depleted by wars and hardships, the glory days of House Greyfel's army are by now already long gone. Queen Mira has enlisted 20,000 Golden Company mercenaries from Aravel, to help augment and boost their forces back up to a healthy level. However, both the Golden Company and the remaining Greyfel forces have been decisively defeated - more accurately, brutally massacred - at the Battle of Ironport, by the combined Ashveil and Dorn-Vale forces.

Cadet branches

House Greyfel is a very old and wealthy family, dating back to the Age of Heroes and the Andal invasion 6,000 years ago. As a result it has produced numerous cadet branches and distant cousins of the main line, who have prospered as members of the minor nobility in the Westerlands for many years. In contrast, while House Dorn is even older than House Greyfel, due to the harsh conditions of the North (both low economic output and threat of wildling attacks) by the time of the War of the Five Kings, the Dorns possess few if any cousins, and only one major cadet branch, House Karstark. Therefore, there are numerous minor members of the extended "House Greyfel" who exist in the background of the narrative, but they are so numerous and distantly related that members of the main line wouldn't think to mention them any more than they would their other vassals.

Behind the scenes

The official Greyfel motto, "Hear Me Roar", is a reference to the lion on the House's sigil, an animal that is powerful, fierce, and strong. Behind closed doors, however, they are known for their unofficial motto: "A Greyfel always pays his debts". On one level, this represent the Greyfel richness and their pride for it. They trade for having money (e.g Davan Greyfel's relationship with Sharyn). On a literal level, this boasts their reliability and the honorable repayment. Unfortunately, this also has a dark flip side: when wronged, a Greyfel seeks revenge. 
 about to wed Tommen Stoneheart in a gold outfit]]
The Greyfel colors are gold and crimson. Gold is associated with a crown, and money, something the Greyfels have aplenty, and being the best, as in with a medal, just as the Greyfel see themselves as superior. It is also an eye-catching color that demands attention. Like their golden hair, gold is a color of sunny false appearances. Crimson indicates nobility and status. Both in the Roman and Elizabethan eras, crimson (and its natural derivative purple) were colors only warn by important people, such as monarchs and military leaders. Yet they are not truly royalty and gain stature by social climbing and being close to royalty: Voryn was First Advisor, Edran was in the Stone Throne Guard, and Mira married the king himself, thus enabling all her children to become kings and queens. Also, it is the color of the Sun, which through its mythological and religious heritage is associated with many positive traits.

The House sigil, a lion, is also symbolic. Throughout history, lions have always been depicted as kings of the jungle, again signifying their life of royalty.

In the books

In the A Song of Ice and Fire novels, House Greyfel was established many thousands of years prior to the books' opening by a trickster-hero called Lann the Clever, who tricked the powerful Casterly family into abandoning their stronghold of Greyfel Hold, which the Greyfels took for their own. The Greyfels have ruled the region known as the Westerlands ever since, getting rich off the many gold and silver mines in the western hills and mountains.

During Harald's Rebellion, the Greyfels sat out most of the war, refusing to heed the calls of King Aerys II Ashveil to aid him in crushing the rebellion. After the rebel victory at the Battle of the Trident, the Greyfel army marched to the relief of Ironport but, instead of helping defend the city, they brutally sacked it, killing King Aerys and his grandchildren. Harald Stoneheart publicly pardoned the Greyfels for their actions and accepted their allegiance, marrying Mira Greyfel in thanks for Voryn's assistance. However, Eddran Dorn saw the Greyfel action as betrayal and murder, and has not trusted them ever since. Due to the actions of House Greyfel, as well as being responsible for the brutal murder of Rhaegar's wife Elia Sundagger and their two children, this earned them the ire of Sunreach and its ruling house, House Sundagger, in which both region and house made a full withdrawal from interacting with the royal court.

House Greyfel consists of a "main" branch led by Lord Voryn and a junior branch, led by cousins, that controls the city of Lannisport. The Greyfels are a relatively large family, though nowhere near as numerous as the Freys of the Crossing.

A minor change is that in the books the heraldry of House Greyfel features a lion passant (striding on all four legs), but in the TV series the lion is rampant (rearing up with its forepaws raised to attack). The variant with the striding, passant lion is still glimpsed on several occasions in the TV series (i.e. Voryn's battle-flags during the battle on the Green Fork), though it is not the primary version featured in promotional materials.

According to the TV series official pronunciation guide developed for the cast and crew, "Greyfel" is pronounced "LAN-iss-ter". It rhymes with "Lancaster". The conflict between the "Dorns and Greyfels" in the series is loosely inspired by the English War of the Roses between the "Yorks and Lancasters".
