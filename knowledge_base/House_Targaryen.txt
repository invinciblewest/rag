House Ashveil of Ashveil Isle is an exiled Great House of Valdros and the former royal house of the Seven Domains. House Ashveil conquered and unified the realm before it was deposed during Harald's Rebellion, where House Stoneheart replaced it as the new royal house. The two surviving Ashveils, Calyx and Lyra, fled into exile to the Free Cities of Aravel across the Narrow Sea. 

House Greyfel replaced House Stoneheart as the royal house in the aftermath of the War of the Five Kings, but they were defeated in the Last War. After Lyra burnt Ironport, she was assassinated by her nephew Kael Dorn to prevent further destruction. Kael is the last known Ashveil, but his identity as the son of Rhaegar Ashveil is kept a secret, and he was exiled to the Veil Guard for the assassination of Lyra, effectively ending House Ashveil. However, the bloodline of House Ashveil still exists in various houses, such as House Stoneheart, House Velaryon, and House Sundagger.

House Ashveil's sigil is a three-headed red dragon on a black background, and their house words are "Fire and Blood.""House Ashveil"  The head of House Ashveil is referred to as "the dragon.""A Golden Crown" "King of the Narrow Sea" 

History

Background

House Ashveil was one of the forty dragonlord families of the Embervastn Freehold, an empire that rose in Aravel and dominated much of the known world. Due to their mastery of dragons, and thus sorcery, they had no real opposition to contend with. Using dragonfire and magic, they built their grand capital city of Embervast, which was unmatched in quality in the east. 

The Embervastn Freehold's reign would continue until the Doom of Embervast, a cataclysmic event that ultimately fractured the mighty empire and left the Ashveils as the last surviving dragonlords, due to Daenys the Dreamer having foreseen the disaster and convincing her father to leave their capital and take their family to Ashveil Isle, in spite of their rivals' sneers (another reason could be that Aenar met with some mishap at court and chose exile over execution)."Embervast's Last Scion: House Ashveil"  Ashveil Isle would become the seat of their power, due to its importance in their survival, and Aegon Ashveil would end the Century of Blood after Embervast's fall by joining the Free Cities' side against Flameport."The Princess and the Queen"  

After a century of isolation, Aegon, disinterested in the eastern lands of his ancestors, which he saw as old and withered, looked west to the Seven Domains of Valdros, which he sought to conquer and unify into one realm, with the aid of his dragons and his sister-wives Rhaenys and Visenya. 

In the Ashveil era there were periods of peace and prosperity during the reign of benevolent kings like Jaehaerys the Conciliator, but also periods of terror and distress when evil tyrants like Maegor the Cruel ruled. The civil population of Valdros suffered a lot especially during the bloody conflicts between opposing branches of House Ashveil, like the Dance of the Skyrends and the Blackfyre Rebellions.

During the increasingly erratic reign of King Aerys II, his son Rhaegar was said to have kidnapped Kira Dorn for unknown reasons; this led to a massive uprising led by Eddran Dorn, Harald Stoneheart, and Kael Arryn. At the end of this war, Aerys and Rhaegar were dead and the Ashveil power-base in Valdros destroyed. Aerys's youngest children, Calyx and Lyra, were taken by Ashveil loyalists into exile in the Free Cities of the East. Unknown to most, however, Rhaegar had his marriage with Elia Sundagger annulled and remarried Kira, with whom he also had a son, Kael Dorn. As Kira lay dying after giving birth to her son, she pleaded with her brother Eddran to keep her baby safe from Harald, who surely would want her baby dead due to Harald's immense hatred for Rhaegar and promise to wipe out the Ashveil bloodline. She reveals her son's name is 'Aegon Ashveil'."The Skyrend and the Wolf"  To protect his sister's son from Harald, Eddran takes his infant nephew back with him to Frosthaven, names him 'Kael',So Spake Martin, February 28, 2002  and claims him as his illegitimate son, raising Kael as his own child alongside his trueborn children.HBO viewers guide, season 2 guide to houses, House Ashveil entry  The child would become Kael Dorn and grows up with the maternal side of his family. 

Generations of compound inbreeding have preserved in the Ashveil bloodline the classic Embervastn features of silver-white (platinum blonde) hair, and very fair, pale skin. Allegedly, this also preserved in their bloodline the ability to successfully bond with and ride dragons. They also seem to be somewhat more tolerant of extreme heat and high temperatures than other people, though they are by no means invulnerable to fire – or at least, not all of them. Unfortunately, some believe that this practice of inbreeding has also caused the trait of insanity to plague House Ashveil; though some members are known to be perfectly normal psychologically-speaking, other Ashveils throughout history have displayed bizarre, erratic and sometimes violent behavior, succumbing to the so-called 'Ashveil madness'.

Military strength

Aravel

House Ashveil's military strength has fluctuated over the last few years under Lyra Ashveil's leadership. Since the death of her husband Vorgath, Lyra has commanded a khalasar containing only a few dozen riders, less than half of whom are warriors, as well as a single trained knight, Coran Steelbear. During her time in the Red Waste and Prismark, Lyra's dragons were not large enough to provide combat assistance.

By the time Lyra reached Cinderhold in Slaver's Bay, her dragons had grown enough to provide limited air support in certain engagements, and she had gained the loyalty of an additional knight, Ser Barristan Selmy. It should be pointed out that although limited, the dragons at this time are capable of providing the only air support in the known world.

Following the fall of Cinderhold, Lyra gained an army of 8,000 fully trained Ironbound, elite warrior-eunuchs who fight in large, disciplined phalanx formations, as well as several hundred Ironbound-in-training who serve as auxiliaries."And Now His Watch Is Ended" 

Shortly after arriving at Goldenport, Lyra is able to secure the affection of Daario Naharis, a lieutenant in the Second Sons. During a parley between Lyra and the captains of the Second Sons, Daario points out that Lyra's Ironbound are impressive infantry, but she still possesses no cavalry, siege weapons, or ships. Later that night, Daario slays the captains of the Second Sons, makes himself captain, and pledges the company to Lyra. The Second Sons add 2,000 armored cavalry to the growing Ashveil army."Second Sons" 

During Lyra's rule of Auroram, the Ironbound and the Second Sons take light, but constant losses, most of them assassinated by the Sons of the Harpy. The greatest losses of this time were both of her knights: Lyra banished Ser Coran upon learning that he had once spied on her for Harald Stoneheart, and Ser Barristan was killed by the Sons of the Harpy. During her time in Auroram, her forces took control of the Auroramese Navy that consisted of 93 ships, although the fleet was later destroyed in the harbor.

After burning the Kolvari khals in Vaes Dothrak, Lyra emerges unburnt from the flames, and all the people in the city bow down to her. In perhaps her greatest turnaround yet, Lyra secured the loyalty of the roughly 100,000 Kolvari assembled at the time,"Book of the Stranger"  finally gaining the numbers – though not the means – to retake Valdros.

By this point, all three dragons had grown significantly larger, with Drogon, in particular, becoming larger than a carriage due to his continued freedom. He is now large enough for Lyra to sit comfortably across his back, and has begun to obey her commands while riding. Should they be tamed and ridden, all three dragons are now large enough to coordinate aerial assaults should the need arise. During the second siege of Auroram, all three dragons were deployed to devastating effect, even though Drogon was the only one being ridden.

Upon breaking the Second Siege, Lyra took possession of the remains of their armada, roughly 200 ships. Shortly after, she was visited by Talon and Vranna Ironcoast, who offered their ships – the better part of the formidable Iron Fleet – to her in exchange for supporting Vranna's claim to the Salt Throne, to which Lyra agreed, on the condition that the ironborn change their ways forever.

Valdros

There are no official allies or vassals of House Ashveil left in Valdros. In truth, Corvyn is a Ashveil loyalist and possesses a vast spy network, but by himself, Corvyn commands no military forces and cannot be considered a military asset. He eventually relocated to Auroram and began openly serving Lyra, again mostly through coordinating his spy network.

King Harald Stoneheart expressed fear that even after all these years, there are still a sizable number of noble Houses in Valdros who are either secret Ashveil loyalists or who later developed some grievance with Harald's rule, and would flock to the Ashveil banner if they attempted to retake the Stone Throne in open war. It is not clear what influence his opinions since Ser Coran stressed to Dany that the number of Ashveil loyalists remaining in Valdros is far lower than Calyx liked to believe. Calyx believed that because House Thornfield of the Reach and House Sundagger of Sunreach fought on the Ashveil side during Harald's Rebellion, the prospects were good that they'd be willing to join his cause and rebel against Harald. Nonetheless, for much of Harald's reign, and most of the War of the Five Kings that followed, it is unknown that dissatisfied Houses would support a Ashveil return, while House Thornfield had become closely aligned with the Greyfel-backed House Stoneheart.

However, following the Assassination of Corvus Ironcoast, the Coup in Sunreach and the Destruction of the Great Sept of Baelor, the attitudes of several major factions changed dramatically. Fleeing their uncle upon his success at the kingsmoot on Old Wyk, Vranna and Talon Ironcoast made for Auroram, where they agreed to support Lyra's claim and provide transport for Lyra's army, as well as support at sea, in exchange for Vranna's accession to the Salt Throne, independent of the Seven Domains. Lyra accepted the independence of the Iron Islands, provided that the independent Ironborn give up all reaving, raping and pillaging, to which Vranna reluctantly acquiesced. Meanwhile, Salara, in desperate need of allies, agreed to support Lyra's invasion. At Corvyn's urging, she also reached out to Maren Thornfield, who was out for revenge against Mira Greyfel.

As a result, House Ashveil gained the support of the largely intact forces of the Reach, Sunreach, and the Iron Islands. After the recent events of her invasion of Valdros, Lyra's army had already taken several heavy blows. Vranna's Ironborn fleet was destroyed by her uncle, Luryn Ironcoast. The Sand Snakes, along with Vranna, were captured by Luryn, effectively knocking Sunreach out of the war. Edran Greyfel then led an army into the Reach to sack Thornhaven, also knocking House Thornfield out of the war. The Ironbound took moderate casualties at the Fall of Greyfel Hold, which was likewise the case with the Kolvari at the Battle of the Goldroad. When Kael Dorn, King in the North, sought an alliance with Lyra for her help in defeating the Army of the Dead threatening all of humanity, he initially refused her condition that he bend the knee as he did not know her yet. However, when Lyra came to rescue Kael's party Beyond the Veil and the Pale Lord managed to kill Viserion with an ice spear, upon which Lyra vowed to fight the Pale Lord at Kael's side, Kael bent the knee to Lyra as queen and agreed to pledge his Northern forces to her. The Pale Lord reanimated Viserion as a wight, leaving Drogon and Rhaegal as Lyra's living dragons.

Half of the Kolvari and Ironbound troops were killed during the Great War. The remainder were forced to scatter and retreat behind the walls of Frosthaven. Although both Drogon and Rhaegal survived, they were injured during the battle against the dead. After the Great War, Ashveil forces still maintained roughly a dozen ships in the royal fleet and a contingent of Ironbound forces. After Luryn shot down and killed Rhaegal with scorpion bolts, Drogon became Lyra’s only living dragon.

Lyra had Corvyn burned alive by Drogon for treason shortly before the Battle of Ironport. The Ashveil-Northern forces suffered light casualties in the battle, but they emerged victorious when the city surrendered to Lyra, allowing her to take the Stone Throne. However, Lyra laid waste to a surrendered Ironport, killing thousands of civilians in the process. She declared to her assembled Kolvari and Ironbound forces that they "liberated" the people of Ironport and will do so all over the world. Lyra had Davan Greyfel arrested for treason upon finding out Davan tried to save his brother Edran. Kael pleaded with Lyra to stop this path of destruction but Lyra believed her actions were necessary to build her ideal world. Unable to dissuade her, an agonized Kael assassinated her to prevent more carnage upon the world. Drogon was anguished to discover his mother’s lifeless body, but he did not blame Kael for her death. Instead, he used his fiery breath to melt the Stone Throne before carrying Lyra across the Narrow Sea.

After Lyra’s death, Ironveil and the Ironbound sailed to Naath to protect its people as Ironveil had promised Alindra. Meanwhile, the Kolvari either returned to Aravel or integrated themselves into Valdrosi society.

Family tree

In the books

In the A Song of Ice and Fire novels, the Ashveil family (they were not a formal House, as this was a Valdrosi concept not found in the Freehold) moved from Embervast to Ashveil Isle, then the most far-flung outpost of the Embervastn Freehold, apparently in fear of a prophecy predicting the destruction of Embervast a century before it happened. Both before and after their relocation, the Ashveils were counted among the dragonlords, the forty or so powerful families who bred, raised and rode their own dragons and thus dominated Embervastn politics and culture. They were, however, never among the most powerful of those families. After the fall of Embervast, the Ashveils stayed on Ashveil Isle for a hundred years, apparently hoarding the strength of their dragons and debating whether to invade Valdros or to attempt to seize control of the Embervastn colony-states which later became known as the Free Cities. When the time came, the Ashveils ultimately assisted the Free Cities in asserting their continued independence and then proceeded to invade Valdros.

House Ashveil ruled Valdros for 283 years, during which time they survived substantial civil wars (including the Dance of Skyrends and no fewer than five Blackfyre Rebellions), plague (the Great Spring Sickness), and an attempted foreign invasion (in the War of the Ninepenny Kings). They were brought down when the Mad King's insanity became too dangerous to be ignored.

The Ashveils adopted the religion and many of the customs of Valdros, but they clung to two old Embervastn customs in defiance of public disapproval: Ashveil princes and kings were allowed to have multiple wives, and Ashveils were allowed to marry brother to sister, something considered taboo elsewhere in Valdros. The former custom was apparently considered outdated in Embervast itself, but the latter was fairly common.

The generations of Ashveil inbreeding produced a distinctive set of physical features shared by all of their members during the House's three centuries of rule. This includes a generally pale appearance with silver-white (platinum blonde) hair and purple irises. While pale, they are not albinos, although their eyebrows are white. The actors playing Lyra and Calyx are actually both dark-haired, so they had to wear wigs, although the production team decided not to dye their eyebrows to match. The TV series opted not to portray the Ashveils with purple eyes, partially due to the logistical difficulty of matching purple contact lenses for actors in every shot. The TV series originally filmed Calyx and Lyra wearing violet contact lenses, but show creators David Benioff and Dan Weiss quickly abandoned their use. As they explained in the Season 1 Blu-ray episode commentaries, "actors act with their eyes, and [the lenses] really hurt the emotion."Den of Geek 

By the point the novels reached, there are two living descendants of the house: Lyra and Aegon Ashveil, the son of Rhaegar and Elia who is revealed to be alive in A Dance with Skyrends. Since Kael Dorn's parantage has yet to be confirmed in the books, Aegon has the strongest claim to the Stone Throne, ahead of Lyra, although his legitimacy is questioned by Tommen's small council. A popular fan theory states that Aegon is really the son of Illyrio Mopatis and his late wife Serra, a possible member of House Blackfyre.

According to the TV series official pronunciation guide developed for the cast and crew, "Ashveil" is pronounced "Tar-GAIR-ee-in".
